# Alvin K. Tay, PhD

**Behavioral Scientist · Psychometrics & Computational Measurement · AI Evaluation**

*Understanding human behavior. Measuring artificial intelligence.*

I am a psychological and population scientist, Adjunct Professor at **Teachers College, Columbia University**, and researcher affiliated with **UNSW Sydney**.

For more than 16 years my research has focused on trauma, stress, psychological adaptation, social epidemiology, and the measurement of complex human behavior. I have authored or coauthored more than 100 peer-reviewed publications and led research and implementation projects across international academic and humanitarian settings, including the United Nations.

I am now extending this work into artificial intelligence: building computational measurement tools and investigating how psychological science can inform the evaluation of AI systems.

## What I'm working on

### [Weftwell](https://weftwell.com) · AI Training & Assessment Infrastructure

**Founder & Research Lead**

Infrastructure for training and assessing interpersonal and helping skills through AI-simulated interactions, with structured feedback and competency-oriented assessment.

The project asks how AI systems can scaffold skills development, and how we can establish whether performance in simulated interactions reflects transferable human competence.

Currently in development and beta testing; empirical validation studies are planned.

### [METER](https://meterresearch.ai) · Pretrained Psychometric Inference

**Co-founder & Principal Investigator**

Can a model learn to perform psychometric inference across different instruments and populations without being refitted for every new dataset?

METER explores this question with simulation-trained neural and psychometric models. Initial results show promising transfer to previously unseen instruments, while identifying important boundaries of generalization and measurement validity.

→ [Research preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10910303/v1)
→ [Code and reproducibility package](https://github.com/meter-psychometrics/meter-paper)

## Emerging AI research interests

My research increasingly extends from understanding human behavior to investigating the behavior of artificial intelligence. I am particularly interested in:

- **Model psychology & interpretability** — functional representations of emotion, threat, stress, and moral reasoning in large language models.
- **AI behavioral evaluation** — distinguishing observed model performance from generalizable functional capabilities.
- **AI safety & robustness** — how social pressure, conflicting authority, and psychologically challenging contexts influence model decisions.
- **Computational social science** — social influence, collective behavior, and emergent dynamics in populations of interacting AI agents.
- **Psychometric foundations of AI evaluation** — construct validity, measurement invariance, reliability, and cross-cultural generalization.

The question driving this work:

> **When an AI system appears to reason, understand, or respond appropriately, what evidence would establish the computational capabilities underlying that behavior?**
